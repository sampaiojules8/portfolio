attentive builder

Problem

Routine sign-up unit changes kept landing on engineering. Manual, repetitive work drove up costs and pulled engineers away from higher-impact projects.

Goals

Cut client-side bugs, especially CSS and rendering issues from custom creative code, so units render correctly on every platform and device and clients can trust the tool.

Projected solution

Expand what the builder can do through a more intuitive interface. Automating complex steps and building in customization reduces the errors that come with coding from scratch, and lets internal teams ship more sign-up units, faster.

Early wireframes

I explored several layouts and ran a competitive analysis of tools our clients already knew, so the new builder would match their mental models. I also audited the previous builder to decide what to keep and what to fix.

Reducing cognitive load

Keep what worked and fix what didn't. The first iteration introduced dual side panels, matching industry-standard editors and making the interface easier to scan.

Solving the biggest problem

Clients' top request was reordering components without writing CSS. A drag-and-drop editor removed over 60% of CSS-related use cases, opened the builder to non-technical marketers, and strengthened Attentive's competitive position.

Gathering feedback

Client interviews run through Great Question AI surfaced needs, pain points, and feature expectations. Those insights guided key decisions and supported the launch and adoption of CB3.

The components

Modular building blocks let designers mix and match components to fit each brand while keeping campaigns consistent.

The redesign

Any extra CSS now scales within standardized templates. That structure cuts bugs and rendering issues without taking customization away.

Simplified creatives

Users pick from multiple templates and layout styles and can switch between them instantly, adapting to different brand aesthetics and campaign goals.

Accessibility as the goal

Users can reorder layers so screen readers follow the intended content order instead of the visual layout, making sign-up units work for people using assistive technology.

Before and after

The old builder split work across setup pages, tabs, and nested forms. The redesign puts everything in one editor: layers on the left, the live preview in the middle, and settings for whatever is selected on the right.

Business impact

Templates, layer management, and automated CSS made the builder more flexible, accessible, and efficient. Clients make fewer errors and get higher-quality sign-up units, and Attentive's position in the market is stronger for it.
